# Panel defence

Answered from the implementation. Every answer names the file or the measured
number it rests on.

---

**1. Why does this need a graph?**

Because the pattern has no per-row expression. Three accounts at 6/2, 7/2 and 5/2
are individually unremarkable; together, sharing a device and returning inside one
window, they are one operation.

Caveat stated first: on this held-out split the account baseline caught every
ring, so **the recall advantage is not demonstrated by this build**. What is
demonstrated is precision — the baseline flagged 5 of 12 households, the graph
flagged 0.

---

**2. Your held-out F1 is 1.0. Why should I believe any of this?**

You should not read it as a capability estimate, and the README says so in bold.
A perfect score on synthetic data generated by the same author who chose the
features is a statement about the corpus.

What survives that caveat is the comparison: both detectors saw the same rings,
with the same denominators, and the baseline was tuned on the same split with the
same cost ratio rather than left as a straw man. The gap between 0 and 5
household false positives is the result.

---

**3. How do you avoid flagging families?**

Four ways, all testable. Address sharing is worth 4 points of 100. Structural
signals total 34 and cannot reach the threshold of 70. The structural-only
guardrail explicitly caps any cluster with under 8 behavioural points. And half
the clustered corpus is benign and structurally identical to a ring, so the
false-positive rate measures something.

The `legitimate-household` scenario gives the system a shared card, shared
devices and one address — everything a ring has — and it scores 54.3, not flagged.

---

**4. Shared address, shared device and shared card. Surely that is enough?**

No, and the guardrail makes that structural rather than a matter of tuning. That
description fits a family. Without unusual behaviour alongside it, the score is
capped below the threshold no matter how saturated the structural signals are.
Tested in `tests/unit/scoring.test.ts`.

---

**5. What stops the score being a black box?**

It is a weighted sum over ten bounded features, published in full at `/signals`.
`explainScore()` reconstructs the arithmetic line by line, and a test asserts the
signal points sum exactly to the risk score. The UI never renders a score without
the breakdown beside it.

---

**6. Why not a learned model? It would score better.**

It would, and it could not be decomposed in front of the person being
investigated. Feature attributions on a boosted ensemble approximate the model's
reasoning; a weighted sum's attribution *is* the reasoning. For an output that is
a decision to investigate people, that difference decides it.

---

**7. How is entity resolution done?**

Exact match on an anonymised hash. No edit distance, no near-duplicate detection,
no name similarity. Where the data does not support an exact link, no link is
recorded — because the cost of a wrong merge here is a person investigated for
someone else's behaviour.

It under-links, and that is the deliberate direction to err.

---

**8. How is a derived edge different from a real one?**

`derived: true`, a `viaEntityId` naming the shared node, dashed magenta versus
solid cyan in the graph view, and a click that shows the observation underneath.
A test asserts every derived edge carries both.

---

**9. What stops one shared node collapsing the whole graph?**

The fanout guard: any infrastructure node touched by more than 40 accounts is
skipped. A device seen by 400 accounts is a default value, and connecting them
pairwise would produce 79,800 edges and one mega-cluster. This actually happened
during the build — see the failure diary.

---

**10. Why two clustering methods?**

They fail in opposite directions. Shared-entity components chain through weak
links; Louvain cuts weak bridges but is order-sensitive. Reporting both is more
informative than picking one and describing its failure in a comment.

---

**11. What is cluster stability and why measure it?**

Modularity has many near-optimal partitions. A cluster that dissolves under a
different node ordering is an artefact of iteration order, and showing it to a
reviewer as "these seven accounts are connected" presents an accident. Measured by
re-clustering under rotations and scoring Jaccard overlap. It lowers confidence and
routes to review; it never raises the score.

---

**12. Why are risk and confidence separate numbers?**

Because "risk 91, confidence 63%" is actionable and a single 77 is not. Confidence
measures input quality and is independent of which way the score points. A test
asserts that identical features with thin versus rich evidence produce the same
risk and different confidence.

---

**13. When does the system refuse to decide?**

Sparse graph, unstable clustering, three or more legitimate explanations,
confidence below threshold, or untrusted content in the metadata. The sparsity
check runs before any risk banding, so a saturated feature vector cannot turn a
sparse cluster into a detection.

---

**14. What is the baseline, and is it a straw man?**

An account-level threshold rule — return rate, refund share, velocity, tenure —
with no access to the graph. It is what most production return-abuse controls
actually are, it catches loud individual abusers perfectly well, and it is tuned
on the same split with the same cost ratio as the graph detector.

It is not the strongest possible baseline. A gradient-boosted account-level
classifier would be harder, and the README lists that as a limitation.

---

**15. What does coordination recovery measure, and what did it show?**

Of the rings the account baseline missed, how many the graph recovered. On this
split: **0 out of 0 — the baseline missed nothing.** That is a negative result for
the recall half of the thesis and it is reported rather than dropped, alongside
`graphMissedBaselineCaught`, because a recovery rate quoted without the cases
going the other way is a half-truth.

---

**16. What is the FP cost and the FN cost?**

FP: analyst time, plus something this system cannot price — a household
investigated for living together. FN: refund loss and continued abuse. The default
ratio is 6:10, weighted toward avoiding the false positive for that reason, and
configurable because a business's real trade-off is not this system's to assume.

---

**17. How were the splits done, and how do you know they do not leak?**

Ring-level, never row-level: splitting accounts would put ring members in train
and held-out at once. A test asserts every ring lies wholly within one split.
Difficulty is drawn from an independent hash so it cannot correlate with split —
an earlier version derived both from `i % 10` and put every adversarial ring in
one place.

The leakage no split protects against: the feature set was designed by someone who
had seen the whole corpus. That is stated in the threat model.

---

**18. What does the model do?**

Turns computed signals into sentences. It does not see the graph, cannot introduce
a signal the scorer did not compute, cannot alter a number, cannot state a
verdict. Two rejection conditions are enforced after the call — an uncomputed
signal name, or verdict language — and either discards the whole response.

With `LLM_PROVIDER=none`, as in this build, every number in the console is
deterministic.

---

**19. How do you handle prompt injection?**

Detection over every string leaf to depth 8, quarantine, forced review, and
structural containment. The `prompt-injection` demo produces a risk score
byte-identical to the clean run and still routes to review — the injection asked
for a zero score and no review and got neither.

The detector is regex-based and is described as defence in depth, not a boundary.

---

**20. Could this system be used to help someone evade detection?**

`POST /api/explain` refuses four families of evasion request while allowing "why
was this cluster flagged?". Both sides are tested, including the false-positive
side, because a refusal that blocks the product's own core question is a broken
product.

The stronger point: the weight table is published anyway. A detector whose safety
depends on its weights being secret is not safe.

---

**21. What happens if someone calls the enforcement endpoint?**

They get `ENFORCEMENT_REFUSED` with a reason, and the attempt is audited. There is
no enforcement code path. `SENTINEL_MODE` refuses BLOCK, ENFORCE, AUTO_BLOCK,
SUSPEND, LIVE, PRODUCTION and PROD by name, so a misconfigured deployment fails to
boot rather than acting on a graph score.

---

**22. What PII does this hold?**

Only anonymised hashes. No column contains a raw address, device id or card
number. A test asserts no PAN-shaped string appears in a generated corpus.

The honest limit: hashes are pseudonymous, not anonymous. An adversary with the
original values can confirm membership by hashing them. The mitigation is that
this system never needs the originals.

---

**23. What is the biggest security gap in this build?**

There is no per-merchant authorisation and no read-side authentication. Every
cluster is visible to every caller. For a product whose artefact is a map of who
is connected to whom, that is the most significant gap, and it is a build
limitation rather than a design position.

---

**24. How would you deploy this against real data?**

Point `DATABASE_URL` at PostgreSQL — the migrations are dialect-identical. Then,
in order: add read-side authorisation per merchant; re-derive the population
baselines from real return behaviour, because thresholds calibrated to the wrong
population are how a detector starts flagging ordinary customers; re-measure the
hard-negative rates against real households, which this corpus only approximates;
and surface fanout-skipped nodes for manual inspection instead of dropping them.

---

**25. What is the largest limitation?**

That the corpus is synthetic and the held-out score is perfect, which together
mean the absolute numbers describe the generator. The comparison against the
baseline survives that, and the household false-positive gap is the finding — but
anyone reading 100% precision as a capability estimate is reading it wrong, and
the README says so before it says anything else about the numbers.
